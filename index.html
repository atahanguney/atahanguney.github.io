01About

About me

I'm Atahan Güney, a 3rd-year student in the Department of Electrical and Electronics Engineering at Antalya Bilim University. Next to the degree I also study computer programming, which is probably why most of my projects end up half hardware and half software.

The subjects I enjoy most are electromagnetics and anything to do with signals: how a wave loses energy in a lossy medium, why a mismatched load gives a standing wave, how much filtering a noisy sensor really needs. I understand these best when I can change a parameter and see what happens, so I tend to write small tools for it. ElectroField is the biggest of those so far, and SerialScope came out of the microcontroller labs.

For hardware I mostly use ESP32, Raspberry Pi Pico 2W and Arduino boards. A good part of the time goes into the less exciting parts: soldering, checking things with a multimeter, getting a breadboard circuit to behave, and making the serial link between the board and the PC reliable.

02Software

Projects

Web app · Python engine in the browser

ElectroField

ElectroField is an interactive workbench for applied electromagnetics, using the notation of Ulaby & Ravaioli and Cheng. You change ε, μ, σ, the frequency or the geometry, and a Python engine (NumPy/SciPy) recomputes the results and writes out the derivation step by step with the numbers substituted. The part I care about most is verification: every result is also checked in a second, independent way, for example a closed-form formula against a complex square root, Zin against an ABCD-matrix cascade, or the fields put back into Maxwell's equations to check the residual. If a check fails, the page shows it next to the numbers instead of hiding it.

  • Topics: coordinate systems, electrostatics, magnetostatics, Maxwell's equations, plane waves, reflection and transmission, transmission lines and the Smith chart, waveguides and antennas.
  • Runs in the browser: the same Python code runs client-side with Pyodide (WebAssembly), so nothing needs to be installed. Locally it runs on a FastAPI server.
  • For practice: 86 worked problems I wrote in the style of the textbooks (my own wording, with section references), a question bank with randomised values, and a built-in tutor that only uses numbers the engine computed.
  • Testing: 439 pytest tests against textbook closed forms and limiting cases, and a verification report comparing 30 reference problems with the site's numbers (downloadable as CSV).

Desktop tool · UART telemetry & DSP

SerialScope

I made SerialScope because of a problem I kept running into in microcontroller labs. The sensor data was noisy, and every time I wanted to try a different filter or change a PID setpoint I had to edit the C code and flash the board again. SerialScope moves that part to the PC: it filters the incoming data in real time and lets me send new parameters back to the board while it is still streaming.

  • Real-time low-pass filter (EMA): raw and filtered channels on the same axes, α adjustable live.
  • Two-way control: sliders and macros send plain ASCII commands such as SETPOINT:25.0, step pulses and E-stop over UART.
  • Threshold trigger with a visual alarm, and CSV export for MATLAB, Excel or Python.
  • Virtual MCU mode: a simulated noisy sensor, so the filter and control features can be tried without any board connected.

03Hardware & IoT

Hardware projects

HW-01

Computer-vision gesture → servo control

Webcam→Python + OpenCV→UART→Arduino→Servo

Tracks my hand in the webcam image with OpenCV and turns its position into servo angles, which are sent to an Arduino over UART. Making the serial communication reliable and debugging the circuit on the breadboard was a big part of the work, not only the vision code.

HW-02

BLE notification display

Phone→BLE→ESP32 · MicroPython→I²C→SSD1306 OLED

An ESP32 running MicroPython receives notifications from my phone over Bluetooth Low Energy and shows them on a small SSD1306 OLED screen over I²C. A simple project, but a good way to learn how BLE and I²C actually work on a real board.

HW-03

Telegram-controlled hardware & thermal logging

Telegram bot→Wi-Fi→Pico 2W→Relay→Morse audio

A Raspberry Pi Pico 2W / Raspberry Pi project where I send text commands to a Telegram bot and the board converts them to Morse code, switched through a relay module. I also wrote a script that logs the board's CPU temperature together with the ambient temperature and plots them, to compare the two.
